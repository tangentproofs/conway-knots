    Normal forms

    Standard form, continued-fraction form, and canonical form are reached by flypes (as isotopies proved in the paper, not via Tait). The arithmetic continued fraction of a rational has a unique canonical form (Proposition 3), which is the numerical counterpart of the tangle canonical form and is used in Theorem 3.

    Every rational tangle can be brought via isotopy to standard form.

    Theorem173Every rational tangle has a continued fraction form#✓fully proved

    Every rational tangle can be written in continued fraction form.

    The argument reduces to standard form (Lemma 3) and then applies the isotopy identities of Lemma 4, $$ T\ast[n]^{-1} \;\sim\; \bigl([n]+T^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}, $$ repeatedly, converting a standard-form tangle into \([[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\). Thus continued-fraction form and standard form are equivalent presentations.

    Theorem174Every rational tangle isotopes to canonical form#✓fully proved

    Every rational tangle can be isotoped to canonical form: an alternating continued-fraction form of odd length.

    If a continued-fraction vector is not termwise of one sign, a transfer move (Figure 14) together with flypes and flips removes a mixed-sign adjacent pair and reduces the sum of absolute values of the entries, so induction produces an alternating tangle. Length is then made odd by splitting the last term as in Definition 6.

    Theorem172Unique canonical continued fraction#✓fully proved

    Every continued fraction \([a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n]\) can be transformed to a unique canonical form, with sign generically equal to the sign of the first non-zero term.

    The reduction copies the transfer arithmetic of Proposition 2: mixed-sign adjacent terms are rewritten so that the sum of absolute values of the entries drops by one. The remaining uniqueness is Euclid's algorithm (Kauffman–Lambropoulou, end of the proof of Proposition 3). Even length is reduced to odd length by the last-term identities of Remark 5.

    This is the arithmetic counterpart of the tangle canonical form, and is the unicity of continued-fraction form used in Theorem 3.

    Theorem175Canonical diagrams of equal fraction are equal#✓fully proved

    Two tangles already in canonical continued-fraction form with the same arithmetic value are the same diagram: uniqueness of the odd-length one-sign expansion (Proposition 3) supplies a unique canonicalCF q, so both diagrams are (canonicalCF q).tangle and hence isotopic. The remaining canonical form of value \(\infty\) is \([\infty]\).

    A standard-form expression with finite fraction \(q\) isotopes to that unique diagram. The reduction is the transfer / last-term rewrite of Proposition 2, shown to preserve the arithmetic value, so the resulting canonical expansion is canonicalCF q rather than an unspecified alternating tangle. Two standard forms of the same finite fraction are therefore isotopic (and likewise for value \(\infty\)).

    This is strictly smaller than Theorem 3: it does not apply to an arbitrary rational tangle, and it does not claim that \(F\) is invariant under leftover Isotopic generators.

    Theorem176Twist expressions of equal fraction are isotopic#✓fully proved

    Two twist-form expressions with the same arithmetical fraction \(F\) have isotopic diagrams, provided each expression's \(F\) agrees with the right-and-bottom evaluation of toStandard.

    The diagrams first isotope to standard form by the slides already in toStandard_isotopic (horizontal and vertical rational flypes, including left addition and top multiplication). Standard forms of a given finite value (or of \(\infty\)) then isotope to one another by uniqueness of the canonical continued fraction. Algebraic \(F\) equals that standard-form value on every expression that never uses a top product: addition is commutative, so left addition is allowed, while the Conway product is not commutative.

    This is strictly smaller than Theorem 3. It does not apply to an arbitrary rational diagram (an Isotopic witness of IsRational), and it does not claim that \(F\) is well-defined along leftover Isotopic generators.

    Theorem167Standard-form value of a twist diagram is well-defined#✓fully proved

    If two twist-form expressions denote the same PD-code, they have the same standard-form evaluation toStandard.fraction, once each expression is slideReady and a non-monochrome coloring of that code satisfies the diagonal-sum rule. The coloring fraction of the diagram equals each expression's standard-form value, so that value is a function of the diagram rather than of the expression tree.

    On right-and-bottom expressions the coloring is constructed by propagating two initial strand colors, so no extra coloring hypothesis remains, and algebraic \(F\) agrees with the standard-form value. On noMulTop slideReady expressions the coloring hypotheses likewise discharge by colorFrom, for both the standard-form and the algebraic value. The mulTop commutativity hypothesis itself holds when the inner fraction is ±1 (the fixed points of inv: both sides reduce to s ± 1), so mulTop over a unit-fraction twist preserves algebraic F — and on the unitMulTop subclass (every top product over a ±1 inner) the toNoMulTop normalization preserves algebraic F throughout, giving a noMulTop expression with identical F for Theorem 3.

    A diagram that is a twist-form PD-code may therefore be assigned this standard-form value via any right-and-bottom parse. An IsRational witness is an isotopy to a twist diagram, not PD-code equality; assigning \(F\) to an arbitrary rational diagram still requires isotopy invariance (Theorem 2).

    This is not Theorem 2, Theorem 3, or Theorem 4.

    If two slideReady twist-form expressions are related by ColoringIsotopy and the source admits a non-monochrome coloring that satisfies the diagonal sum rule, then they have the same standard-form evaluation toStandard.fraction. On right-and-bottom expressions the coloring is colorFrom, so no extra coloring hypothesis remains; the colorFrom discharge now also covers all noMulTop slideReady expressions (fraction_ColoringIsotopy_noMulTop_colorFrom).

    This is not Theorem 2: ColoringIsotopy does not include unrestricted Figure 14 transfers, flype-slides, or invert_cong, and it is not full diagram isotopy. Assigning \(F\) to an arbitrary rational diagram still requires isotopy invariance of \(F\).

    Theorem189Fixed points of the mulTop commutativity equation#✓fully proved

    The mulTop commutativity equation \((s^{-1}+F)^{-1}=(F^{-1}+s)^{-1}\) holds exactly at \(F=\pm 1\) (per sign, hence jointly): \(0\) and \(\infty\) fail by direct computation, and for finite nonzero values injectivity of inv reduces the equation to \(q=q^{-1}\), i.e. \(q^2=1\). Together with the sufficiency direction, the unit-fraction hypothesis for mulTop normalization is therefore both necessary and sufficient on finite values — blocker (b) is closed on the finiteness fragment. The necessity is sharp at expression level: on mulTop (addRight one pos) pos (inner fraction \(2\)), algebraic \(F\) is \(1/3\) while the standard-form evaluation is \(2/3\) (mulTop_two_inner_ne_toStandard), so the agreement hypotheses of twist_same_fraction_isotopic cannot be dropped in general.
