    Rational tangles

    This chapter isolates the combinatorial theory of rational 2-tangles used by Kauffman and Lambropoulou to prove Conway's classification. It begins with Reidemeister moves, 2-tangles and isotopy, builds integer and rational tangles and the flype/flip moves, then matches continued-fraction form of a tangle with the arithmetic continued fraction of its fraction \(F(T)\). The execution plan for the classification is the integral-coloring argument of §5: a coloring fraction \(f\) is a topological invariant and equals \(F\), so \(F\) is an isotopy invariant (Theorem 2) without Tait. Theorem 3 (same fraction implies isotopic) is kept from §4. The main result is that two rational tangles are isotopic if and only if they have the same fraction.

    Definitions

    A 2-tangle is an embedding of two arcs (and possibly circles) in a 3-ball with four endpoints on the boundary. Diagram isotopy is generated by Reidemeister moves in the interior of the disc. Rational tangles are those generated from \([0]\) or \([\infty]\) by consecutive twists of neighbouring endpoints. Flypes and flips are characteristic isotopies used to reach standard, continued- fraction, and canonical form. The fraction \(F(T)\) is the arithmetical evaluation of the twist form.

    Definition1Reidemeister moves#✓fully proved

    A Reidemeister move is one of the three local diagram changes R1, R2, R3 on a tangle (or knot) diagram, performed in the interior of the projection disc and leaving the four endpoints of a 2-tangle fixed.

    • R1. A kink may be added to or removed from a single strand (a twist of writhe \(\pm 1\)).
    • R2. Two strands with two opposite crossings may be pulled apart, or two uncrossed strands may be overlapped to create two opposite crossings.
    • R3. A strand may be slid over (or under) a crossing of two other strands.

    Kauffman–Lambropoulou use these moves without listing the three pictures: isotopy of 2-tangle diagrams is equivalent to a finite sequence of Reidemeister moves in the interior of the disc (Introduction, arXiv PDF p. 2, citing Reidemeister [31]). Section 5 uses the same moves as the generators of diagram isotopy for coloring. This article spells out R1–R3 so that those uses are formalizable leaves rather than an imported axiom.

    Planar isotopy of the diagram (no change of crossing data) is included with the Reidemeister generators.

    Definition22-tangle and isotopy#✓fully proved

    A 2-tangle is an embedding in a 3-ball \(B^3\) of two arcs and a finite number of circles, with the four endpoints of the arcs lying on \(\partial B^3\) in the standard NW/NE/SW/SE positions. (Throughout, “tangle” means a tangle diagram, as in the paper.)

    Two rational tangles \(T,S\) in \(B^3\) are isotopic, written \(T\sim S\), if there is an orientation-preserving self-homeomorphism \(h:(B^3,T)\to(B^3,S)\) that is the identity on the boundary. Equivalently, any two diagrams of \(T\) and \(S\) have identical endpoint configurations on the projection disc and differ by a finite sequence of Reidemeister moves in the interior of the disc.

    The class of 2-tangles is closed under addition \(T+S\), multiplication \(T\ast S\), mirror image \(-T\), rotation \(T^r\) (counterclockwise by \(90^\circ\)), and inversion \(T^i:=-T^r\). Numerator and denominator closures \(N(T)\) and \(D(T)\) produce knots or links; they are used later to form vertex closures, but are not themselves first-milestone targets.

    Definition6Integer and vertical tangles#✓fully proved

    The simplest rational tangles are \([0]\), \([\infty]\), \([+1]\) and \([-1]\). The next simplest are:

    • the integer tangles \([n]\), made of \(n\) horizontal twists, \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\);
    • the vertical tangles \([n]^{-1}\), made of \(n\) vertical twists, \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\).

    Crossing type follows the checkerboard shading of the paper (Figure 2): positive type as in \([+1]\), negative type as in \([-1]\). These sign conventions agree with Ernst–Sumners and are the opposite of Conway 1970; this project follows Kauffman–Lambropoulou.

    In particular \([0]^r=[0]^i=[\infty]\) and \([\infty]^r=[\infty]^i=[0]\), and \(-[n]=[-n]\).

    Definition9Rational tangle#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle in twist form is created by consecutive additions and multiplications by the tangles \([\pm 1]\), starting from \([0]\) or \([\infty]\) (Definition 1). Equivalently (Note 1), it is a proper embedding of two unoriented arcs \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) in a 3-ball \(B^3\) with four endpoints on the boundary for which there exists a homeomorphism of pairs $$ h:(B^3,\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\longrightarrow(D^2\times I,{x,y}\times I) $$ to a trivial tangle.

    Inductively, a twist-form rational tangle is obtained from a previously created rational tangle by adding integer tangles and multiplying by vertical tangles. Addition or multiplication of two non-integer rational tangles need not remain rational.

    This project takes Definition 1 as the working definition of a rational tangle, with the homeomorphism-of-pairs description as the intended topological meaning.

    Definition7Flype#✓fully proved

    A flype is an isotopy of a 2-tangle or a knot applied on a 2-subtangle of the form \([\pm 1]+t\) or \([\pm 1]\ast t\) as in Figure 5 of the paper. It fixes the endpoints of the subtangle on which it is applied. A flype is rational if that 2-subtangle is rational.

    Algebraically (after the flipping lemma), a rational flype is one of $$ [\pm 1]+t \;\sim\; t+[\pm 1] \qquad\text{or}\qquad [\pm 1]\ast t \;\sim\; t\ast[\pm 1]. $$

    A tangle (or knot) diagram is alternating when the crossings alternate from under to over along every arc; equivalently, with the paper's checkerboard shading, when all crossings have the same type. Flypes preserve the alternating structure. Lemma 1 and Corollary 1 of the paper record that every 2-subtangle of a rational tangle in twist form is a rational truncation, so every flype of a rational tangle is rational.

    Definition8Flip#✓fully proved

    A flip is a rotation in space of a 2-tangle by \(180^\circ\).

    • \(T^{h\mathrm{flip}}\) is the horizontal flip: rotation around a horizontal axis in the plane of \(T\).
    • \(T^{v\mathrm{flip}}\) is the vertical flip: rotation around a vertical axis in the plane of \(T\).

    In general a flip switches endpoints and need not be an isotopy of 2-tangles. For rational tangles it is, by the flipping lemma. A flype on a 2-subtangle \(t\) may be written $$ [\pm 1]+t \;\sim\; t^{h\mathrm{flip}}+[\pm 1] \qquad\text{or}\qquad [\pm 1]\ast t \;\sim\; t^{v\mathrm{flip}}\ast[\pm 1]. $$

    Definition10Standard form#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle is in standard form if it is created by consecutive additions of \([\pm 1]\) only on the right (or only on the left) and multiplications by \([\pm 1]\) only at the bottom (or only at the top), starting from \([0]\) or \([\infty]\) (Definition 4).

    Inductively, from a previously created rational tangle \(T\), one either adds an integer tangle on the right, \(T\mapsto T+[\pm k]\), or multiplies by a vertical tangle at the bottom, \(T\mapsto T\ast[n]^{-1}\). The associated integer vector \((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) records the successive blocks of twists; \(n\) may be taken odd.

    Definition11Continued fraction form#✓fully proved

    A continued fraction in integer tangles is an algebraic description of a rational tangle via a continued fraction built from integer tangles \([a_1],\ldots,[a_n]\) with all numerators equal to \(1\): $$ T = [[a_1],[a_2],\ldots,[a_n]] := [a_1] + \cfrac{1}{[a_2]+\cdots+\cfrac{1}{[a_n]}} $$ for \(a_2,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\) and \(n\) even or odd. The leading term \(a_1\) may be zero. A rational tangle so described is in continued fraction form.

    This is the tangle-level counterpart of the arithmetic continued fraction \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]\). Lemma 5 of the paper records the effect of adding \([\pm 1]\), inverting, and mirroring on the continued-fraction vector.

    Definition14Canonical form of a rational tangle#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle \(T=[[\beta_1],[\beta_2],\ldots,[\beta_m]]\) in continued fraction form is in canonical form if \(T\) is alternating (all \(\beta_i\) positive, or all negative) and \(m\) is odd (Definition 6). If \(T\) is alternating and \(m\) is even, the last term may be split by the identity \([\beta_m]=[\mathrm{sign}(\beta_m)\cdot(|\beta_m|-1)]+[\mathrm{sign}(\beta_m)\cdot 1]\) to make the length odd.

    \(T\) is called positive or negative according to the sign of its terms.

    Definition12Arithmetic continued fraction#✓fully proved

    Every rational number may be written as a continued fraction with all numerators equal to \(1\): $$ [a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n] := a_1 + \cfrac{1}{a_2+\cdots+\cfrac{1}{a_n}} $$ for \(a_1\in\mathbb{Z}\) and \(a_2,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\). The leading term \(a_1\) may be zero. The length is \(n\) whether or not \(a_1=0\).

    A continued fraction is termwise positive (resp. negative) if every numerical term is positive (resp. negative). It is in canonical form (Definition 7) if it is termwise positive or negative and the length \(m\) is odd. Even length is reduced to odd length by the last-term identities \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]=[a_1,\ldots,a_n-1,+1]\) for \(a_n>0\) and \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]=[a_1,\ldots,a_n+1,-1]\) for \(a_n<0\).

    The value \(\infty\) is included formally as \(1/0\), matching \(F([\infty])\).

    Mathlib already develops generalised, simple, and regular continued fractions under Algebra.ContinuedFractions (including GenContFract.of and termination iff rationality). That overlap is arithmetic, not a tangle theory; it is recorded as a candidate, not as mathlib: true.

    Definition13Fraction of a rational tangle#✓fully proved

    Let \(T\) be a rational tangle in twist form. The fraction \(F(T)\) is the rational number (or \(\infty\)) obtained by evaluating that expression in ordinary arithmetic, with the tangle product interpreted as $$ x\ast y := \frac{1}{\frac{1}{x}+y}, $$ and with \(F([\infty]):=\infty=1/0\) as a formal expression (Definition 8).

    By Remark 6, if \(T=[[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\) is in continued fraction form then $$ F(T)=[a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n], $$ and this evaluation may be taken as the definition of \(F(T)\). In particular \(F([0])=0\), \(F([\pm 1])=\pm 1\), \(F([\pm k])=\pm k\), and \(F([{\pm k}]^{-1})=1/(\pm k)\).

    Lemma 7 of the paper shows that the twist-form and continued-fraction-form evaluations agree. Lemma 8 records the calculus \(F(T+[\pm 1])=F(T)\pm 1\), \(F(T^{-1})=1/F(T)\), \(F(-T)=-F(T)\).

    Forms

    Standard form, continued-fraction form, and canonical form are reached by flypes (as isotopies proved in the paper, not via Tait). The arithmetic continued fraction of a rational has a unique canonical form (Proposition 3), which is the numerical counterpart of the tangle canonical form and is used in Theorem 3.

    Lemma6Flipping lemma#●ready to prove

    If \(T\) is rational, then (Kauffman–Lambropoulou Lemma 2) $$ T\sim T^{h\mathrm{flip}}, \qquad T\sim T^{v\mathrm{flip}}, \qquad T\sim (T^i)^i=(T^r)^r. $$ In particular inversion is an operation of order two on rational tangles, so the inverse of a rational tangle \(T\) may be written \(T^{-1}\), and \(T^r=-T^{-1}\).

    The Lean theorem currently proves the coloring-honest fragment: planar \(T^{\mathrm{rot}180}\sim T\) (signs kept) and therefore \(T\sim(T^i)^i=(T^r)^r\), together with commutativity of adjoining \([\pm 1]\) via the sign-preserving flype slide. Horizontal/vertical flip are spatial diagram operations (Crossing.switch) and are not Isotopic generators, so \(T\sim T^{h\mathrm{flip}}\) and \(T\sim T^{v\mathrm{flip}}\) are not obtained as constructors of diagram isotopy.

    Lemma7Every rational tangle has a standard form#●proved

    Every rational tangle can be brought via isotopy to standard form.

    Starting from twist form, horizontal and vertical rational flypes (and the commutativity supplied by the flipping lemma) move all horizontal twists to the right and all vertical twists to the bottom.

    Theorem5Every rational tangle has a continued fraction form#●proved

    Every rational tangle can be written in continued fraction form.

    The argument reduces to standard form (Lemma 3) and then applies the isotopy identities of Lemma 4, $$ T\ast[n]^{-1} \;\sim\; \bigl([n]+T^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}, $$ repeatedly, converting a standard-form tangle into \([[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\). Thus continued-fraction form and standard form are equivalent presentations.

    Theorem6Every rational tangle isotopes to canonical form#●proved

    Every rational tangle can be isotoped to canonical form: an alternating continued-fraction form of odd length.

    If a continued-fraction vector is not termwise of one sign, a transfer move (Figure 14) together with flypes and flips removes a mixed-sign adjacent pair and reduces the sum of absolute values of the entries, so induction produces an alternating tangle. Length is then made odd by splitting the last term as in Definition 6.

    Theorem8Unique canonical continued fraction#✓fully proved

    Every continued fraction \([a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n]\) can be transformed to a unique canonical form, with sign generically equal to the sign of the first non-zero term.

    The reduction copies the transfer arithmetic of Proposition 2: mixed-sign adjacent terms are rewritten so that the sum of absolute values of the entries drops by one. The remaining uniqueness is Euclid's algorithm (Kauffman–Lambropoulou, end of the proof of Proposition 3). Even length is reduced to odd length by the last-term identities of Remark 5.

    This is the arithmetic counterpart of the tangle canonical form, and is the unicity of continued-fraction form used in Theorem 3.

    Classification

    Theorem 2 follows from Theorem 4 (\(f\) invariant and \(f=F\)). Theorem 3 is unchanged from §4. Theorem 1 is their conjunction.

    Theorem7The fraction is an isotopy invariant#●ready to state

    The fraction \(F\) is an isotopy invariant of rational tangles: if \(T\sim S\) are rational, then \(F(T)=F(S)\).

    This is Theorem 2. The proof used in this DAG is the coloring argument of §5, not the flyping argument of §4. By Theorem 4, \(f\) is a topological invariant of rational tangles and \(f(T)=F(T)\). Therefore \(F\) is an isotopy invariant.

    There is no appeal to the Tait flyping conjecture or to Proposition 4.

    Theorem9Same fraction implies isotopic#●ready to state

    Two rational tangles with the same fraction are isotopic.

    Let \(T=[[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\) and \(S=[[b_1],\ldots,[b_m]]\) satisfy \(F(T)=F(S)=p/q\). Bring each to canonical form \(T'\) and \(S'\). Invariance of \(F\) gives \(F(T')=F(S')=p/q\). Proposition 3 supplies a unique arithmetic canonical expansion \(p/q=[\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r]\), hence a unique alternating rational tangle \(Q=[[\gamma_1],\ldots,[\gamma_r]]\) in canonical form. If \(T'\neq Q\) then two distinct canonical continued fractions would evaluate to \(p/q\), contradicting Proposition 3. Thus \(T'=Q=S'\), so \(T\sim S\).

    Theorem10Classification of rational tangles#●planned

    Two rational tangles are isotopic if and only if they have the same fraction.

    This is Theorem 1 of Kauffman–Lambropoulou, attributed to Conway (1970). The paper states it in the Introduction. This DAG obtains it as the conjunction of Theorem 2 (the fraction is an isotopy invariant, via the coloring fraction \(f\) of Theorem 4) and Theorem 3 (equal fractions yield isotopic tangles, from §4). Tait flyping and Proposition 4 are not used.

    Note 2 then records the bijection: if \(T=[[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\) and \(p/q=[a_1,\ldots,a_n]\), one may write \(T=[p/q]\) without ambiguity. Rational numbers (together with \(\infty\)) are thus represented bijectively by rational tangles; negatives correspond to mirrors and inverses to inverses. That bijection is a corollary of this theorem, not a separate first-milestone target.

    The §4 flyping proof of Theorem 2, which uses the Tait conjecture as background, is retained as exposition and is marked out of the coverage contract for this milestone. Nothing on the path to Theorem 1 depends on these articles.

    Theorem3Tait flyping conjecture#●ready to state

    Two alternating knots are isotopic if and only if any two corresponding diagrams on \(S^2\) are related by a finite sequence of flypes.

    This is Tait's 1877 working assumption, proved by Menasco and Thistlethwaite in 1993. Kauffman–Lambropoulou assume the corresponding statement for vertex closures of alternating 2-tangles (citing Thistlethwaite) and derive from it the flyping statement for alternating rational tangles (Proposition 4). The result is background, not a theorem proved in the adopted paper.

    This article is retained as exposition of the unused §4 flyping argument. It is not a dependency of Theorem 1: the classification is proved via the coloring fraction of §5. Tait is not an axiom on the critical path.

    Theorem4Alternating rational tangles related by flypes#●ready to state

    Two alternating rational tangles on \(S^2\) are isotopic if and only if they differ by a finite sequence of rational flypes.

    The argument passes to the vertex closure \(V(T)\) of an alternating rational tangle, invokes the Tait flyping theorem for that closure, and reconfigures any flype that involves the rigid vertex as a pancake flip plus a tangle flype. Pancake flips induce horizontal or vertical flips, which are isotopies of rational tangles by the flipping lemma; remaining tangle flypes are rational by Corollary 1.
