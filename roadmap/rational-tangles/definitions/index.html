    Tangle foundations

    Definitions for the combinatorial theory of rational 2-tangles: Reidemeister moves, 2-tangles and isotopy, integer and rational tangles, flypes and flips, standard, continued-fraction, and canonical forms, the arithmetic continued fraction, and the fraction \(F(T)\) as the arithmetical evaluation of the twist form.

    Definitions

    Inductive1Reidemeister moves#✓fully proved

    A Reidemeister move is one of the three local diagram changes R1, R2, R3 on a tangle (or knot) diagram, performed in the interior of the projection disc and leaving the four endpoints of a 2-tangle fixed.

    • R1. A kink may be added to or removed from a single strand (a twist of writhe \(\pm 1\)).
    • R2. Two strands with two opposite crossings may be pulled apart, or two uncrossed strands may be overlapped to create two opposite crossings.
    • R3. A strand may be slid over (or under) a crossing of two other strands.

    Kauffman–Lambropoulou use these moves without listing the three pictures: isotopy of 2-tangle diagrams is equivalent to a finite sequence of Reidemeister moves in the interior of the disc (Introduction, arXiv PDF p. 2, citing Reidemeister [31]). Section 5 uses the same moves as the generators of diagram isotopy for coloring. This article spells out R1–R3 so that those uses are formalizable leaves rather than an imported axiom.

    Planar isotopy of the diagram (no change of crossing data) is included with the Reidemeister generators.

    Inductive22-tangle and isotopy#✓fully proved

    A 2-tangle is an embedding in a 3-ball \(B^3\) of two arcs and a finite number of circles, with the four endpoints of the arcs lying on \(\partial B^3\) in the standard NW/NE/SW/SE positions. (Throughout, “tangle” means a tangle diagram, as in the paper.)

    Two rational tangles \(T,S\) in \(B^3\) are isotopic, written \(T\sim S\), if there is an orientation-preserving self-homeomorphism \(h:(B^3,T)\to(B^3,S)\) that is the identity on the boundary. Equivalently, any two diagrams of \(T\) and \(S\) have identical endpoint configurations on the projection disc and differ by a finite sequence of Reidemeister moves in the interior of the disc.

    The class of 2-tangles is closed under addition \(T+S\), multiplication \(T\ast S\), mirror image \(-T\), rotation \(T^r\) (counterclockwise by \(90^\circ\)), and inversion \(T^i:=-T^r\). Numerator and denominator closures \(N(T)\) and \(D(T)\) produce knots or links; they are used later to form vertex closures, but are not themselves first-milestone targets.

    Definition3Integer and vertical tangles#✓fully proved

    The simplest rational tangles are \([0]\), \([\infty]\), \([+1]\) and \([-1]\). The next simplest are:

    • the integer tangles \([n]\), made of \(n\) horizontal twists, \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\);
    • the vertical tangles \([n]^{-1}\), made of \(n\) vertical twists, \(n\in\mathbb{Z}\).

    Crossing type follows the checkerboard shading of the paper (Figure 2): positive type as in \([+1]\), negative type as in \([-1]\). These sign conventions agree with Ernst–Sumners and are the opposite of Conway 1970; this project follows Kauffman–Lambropoulou.

    In particular \([0]^r=[0]^i=[\infty]\) and \([\infty]^r=[\infty]^i=[0]\), and \(-[n]=[-n]\).

    The stacked diagram of \(|n|\) vertical twists below \([\infty]\) is the right-and-bottom picture of Conway \([n]^i=1/[n]\). It is not the PD-code of inverting \([n]\), which mirrors every crossing.

    Definition5Rational tangle#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle in twist form is created by consecutive additions and multiplications by the tangles \([\pm 1]\), starting from \([0]\) or \([\infty]\) (Definition 1). Equivalently (Note 1), it is a proper embedding of two unoriented arcs \(\alpha_1,\alpha_2\) in a 3-ball \(B^3\) with four endpoints on the boundary for which there exists a homeomorphism of pairs $$ h:(B^3,\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\longrightarrow(D^2\times I,{x,y}\times I) $$ to a trivial tangle.

    Inductively, a twist-form rational tangle is obtained from a previously created rational tangle by adding integer tangles and multiplying by vertical tangles. Addition or multiplication of two non-integer rational tangles need not remain rational.

    This project takes Definition 1 as the working definition of a rational tangle, with the homeomorphism-of-pairs description as the intended topological meaning.

    Inductive3Flype#✓fully proved

    A flype is an isotopy of a 2-tangle or a knot applied on a 2-subtangle of the form \([\pm 1]+t\) or \([\pm 1]\ast t\) as in Figure 5 of the paper. It fixes the endpoints of the subtangle on which it is applied. A flype is rational if that 2-subtangle is rational.

    Algebraically (after the flipping lemma), a rational flype is one of $$ [\pm 1]+t \;\sim\; t+[\pm 1] \qquad\text{or}\qquad [\pm 1]\ast t \;\sim\; t\ast[\pm 1]. $$

    A tangle (or knot) diagram is alternating when the crossings alternate from under to over along every arc; equivalently, with the paper's checkerboard shading, when all crossings have the same type. Flypes preserve the alternating structure. Lemma 1 and Corollary 1 of the paper record that every 2-subtangle of a rational tangle in twist form is a rational truncation, so every flype of a rational tangle is rational.

    Definition4Flip#✓fully proved

    A flip is a rotation in space of a 2-tangle by \(180^\circ\).

    • \(T^{h\mathrm{flip}}\) is the horizontal flip: rotation around a horizontal axis in the plane of \(T\).
    • \(T^{v\mathrm{flip}}\) is the vertical flip: rotation around a vertical axis in the plane of \(T\).

    In general a flip switches endpoints and need not be an isotopy of 2-tangles. For rational tangles it is, by the flipping lemma. A flype on a 2-subtangle \(t\) may be written $$ [\pm 1]+t \;\sim\; t^{h\mathrm{flip}}+[\pm 1] \qquad\text{or}\qquad [\pm 1]\ast t \;\sim\; t^{v\mathrm{flip}}\ast[\pm 1]. $$

    Definition6Standard form#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle is in standard form if it is created by consecutive additions of \([\pm 1]\) only on the right (or only on the left) and multiplications by \([\pm 1]\) only at the bottom (or only at the top), starting from \([0]\) or \([\infty]\) (Definition 4).

    Inductively, from a previously created rational tangle \(T\), one either adds an integer tangle on the right, \(T\mapsto T+[\pm k]\), or multiplies by a vertical tangle at the bottom, \(T\mapsto T\ast[n]^{-1}\). The associated integer vector \((a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) records the successive blocks of twists; \(n\) may be taken odd.

    Definition7Continued fraction form#✓fully proved

    A continued fraction in integer tangles is an algebraic description of a rational tangle via a continued fraction built from integer tangles \([a_1],\ldots,[a_n]\) with all numerators equal to \(1\): $$ T = [[a_1],[a_2],\ldots,[a_n]] := [a_1] + \cfrac{1}{[a_2]+\cdots+\cfrac{1}{[a_n]}} $$ for \(a_2,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\) and \(n\) even or odd. The leading term \(a_1\) may be zero. A rational tangle so described is in continued fraction form.

    This is the tangle-level counterpart of the arithmetic continued fraction \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]\). Lemma 5 of the paper records the effect of adding \([\pm 1]\), inverting, and mirroring on the continued-fraction vector.

    Definition9Canonical form of a rational tangle#✓fully proved

    A rational tangle \(T=[[\beta_1],[\beta_2],\ldots,[\beta_m]]\) in continued fraction form is in canonical form if \(T\) is alternating (all \(\beta_i\) positive, or all negative) and \(m\) is odd (Definition 6). If \(T\) is alternating and \(m\) is even, the last term may be split by the identity \([\beta_m]=[\mathrm{sign}(\beta_m)\cdot(|\beta_m|-1)]+[\mathrm{sign}(\beta_m)\cdot 1]\) to make the length odd.

    \(T\) is called positive or negative according to the sign of its terms.

    Structure2Arithmetic continued fraction#✓fully proved

    Every rational number may be written as a continued fraction with all numerators equal to \(1\): $$ [a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n] := a_1 + \cfrac{1}{a_2+\cdots+\cfrac{1}{a_n}} $$ for \(a_1\in\mathbb{Z}\) and \(a_2,\ldots,a_n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\). The leading term \(a_1\) may be zero. The length is \(n\) whether or not \(a_1=0\).

    A continued fraction is termwise positive (resp. negative) if every numerical term is positive (resp. negative). It is in canonical form (Definition 7) if it is termwise positive or negative and the length \(m\) is odd. Even length is reduced to odd length by the last-term identities \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]=[a_1,\ldots,a_n-1,+1]\) for \(a_n>0\) and \([a_1,\ldots,a_n]=[a_1,\ldots,a_n+1,-1]\) for \(a_n<0\).

    The value \(\infty\) is included formally as \(1/0\), matching \(F([\infty])\).

    Mathlib already develops generalised, simple, and regular continued fractions under Algebra.ContinuedFractions (including GenContFract.of and termination iff rationality). That overlap is arithmetic, not a tangle theory; it is recorded as a candidate, not as mathlib: true.

    Definition8Fraction of a rational tangle#✓fully proved

    Let \(T\) be a rational tangle in twist form. The fraction \(F(T)\) is the rational number (or \(\infty\)) obtained by evaluating that expression in ordinary arithmetic, with the tangle product interpreted as $$ x\ast y := \frac{1}{\frac{1}{x}+y}, $$ and with \(F([\infty]):=\infty=1/0\) as a formal expression (Definition 8).

    By Remark 6, if \(T=[[a_1],\ldots,[a_n]]\) is in continued fraction form then $$ F(T)=[a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n], $$ and this evaluation may be taken as the definition of \(F(T)\). In particular \(F([0])=0\), \(F([\pm 1])=\pm 1\), \(F([\pm k])=\pm k\), and \(F([{\pm k}]^{-1})=1/(\pm k)\).

    Lemma 7 of the paper shows that the twist-form and continued-fraction-form evaluations agree. Lemma 8 records the calculus \(F(T+[\pm 1])=F(T)\pm 1\), \(F(T^{-1})=1/F(T)\), \(F(-T)=-F(T)\).
