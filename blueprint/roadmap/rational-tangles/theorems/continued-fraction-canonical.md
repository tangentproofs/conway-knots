# Unique canonical continued fraction

Every continued fraction $[a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n]$ can be transformed to a
**unique** canonical form, with sign generically equal to the sign of the
first non-zero term.

The reduction copies the transfer arithmetic of Proposition 2: mixed-sign
adjacent terms are rewritten so that the sum of absolute values of the
entries drops by one. The remaining uniqueness is Euclid's algorithm
(Kauffman–Lambropoulou, end of the proof of Proposition 3). Even length is
reduced to odd length by the last-term identities of Remark 5.

This is the arithmetic counterpart of the tangle canonical form, and is the
unicity of continued-fraction form used in Theorem 3.
