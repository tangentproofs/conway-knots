# Rational tangles

This chapter isolates the combinatorial theory of rational 2-tangles used by
Kauffman and Lambropoulou to prove Conway's classification. It begins with
Reidemeister moves, 2-tangles and isotopy, builds integer and rational
tangles and the flype/flip moves, then matches continued-fraction form of a
tangle with the arithmetic continued fraction of its fraction $F(T)$. The
execution plan for the classification is the integral-coloring argument of
§5: a coloring fraction $f$ is a topological invariant and equals $F$, so
$F$ is an isotopy invariant (Theorem 2) without Tait. Theorem 3 (same
fraction implies isotopic) is kept from §4. The main result is that two
rational tangles are isotopic if and only if they have the same fraction.

## Definitions

A 2-tangle is an embedding of two arcs (and possibly circles) in a 3-ball with
four endpoints on the boundary. Diagram isotopy is generated by Reidemeister
moves in the interior of the disc. Rational tangles are those generated from
$[0]$ or $[\infty]$ by consecutive twists of neighbouring endpoints. Flypes
and flips are characteristic isotopies used to reach standard, continued-
fraction, and canonical form. The fraction $F(T)$ is the arithmetical
evaluation of the twist form.

The [foundations chapter](definitions/README.md) holds the definitions.

## Forms

Standard form, continued-fraction form, and canonical form are reached by
flypes (as isotopies proved in the paper, not via Tait). The arithmetic
continued fraction of a rational has a unique canonical form (Proposition 3),
which is the numerical counterpart of the tangle canonical form and is used
in Theorem 3.

The [normal-forms chapter](theorems/forms/README.md) proves the forms, and
the [toolkit chapter](theorems/README.md) holds the slideReady coloring-fraction
